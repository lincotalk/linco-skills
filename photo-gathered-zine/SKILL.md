# Photo Gathered Zine

Turn one supplied photograph into a vertical 3:5 paper-collage poster. Preserve the scene as evidence; let illustration interpret it. Favor omission, large shapes, and breathable paper over decoration.

## Workflow

1. Confirm that a source photograph is available. Treat an explicit transformation request as consent to use it with the selected image-generation tool. Do not browse, upload elsewhere, or persist the source outside the requested workflow.
2. Read the image before prompting. Record the main subject, one essential spatial relationship, dominant direction, heavy and quiet regions, native palette, and the minimum forms needed to recognize the scene.
3. Choose one layout based on the source geometry: irregular photo anchor, directional split, underprint passage, or transformative seam. Keep the photo at roughly 25–50% of the canvas unless fidelity requires more.
4. Keep the photographic portion truthful. Preserve subject identity, orientation, perspective, relative position, and one source-specific silhouette or gap.
5. Select one primary abstraction grammar: broad silhouette, broken contour, sparse rhythm, atmospheric field, or cut-paper form. Use at most one secondary grammar.
6. Simplify aggressively. Merge repeated leaves, branches, windows, waves, crowds, gravel, and similar detail into a few calm masses. Leave 55–85% of the illustration field unprinted.
7. Extract one shape, path, shadow, gap, or rhythm from the photograph. Use it both to extend the illustration and to carry one high-chroma accent hue across or through the photo–paper boundary.
8. Make the material handoff visible: an asymmetric hand-torn contour, narrow exposed fibers, warm paper showing through, and slight local abrasion. Keep the result flat like a scan; avoid lifted-paper depth.
9. Add micro-text only when the user supplies it or when a tiny, source-aware caption materially helps. Keep it subordinate and reproduce supplied wording verbatim.
10. Generate or edit with a capable raster image tool. For an edit, identify the source as the edit target and repeat the scene invariants in the prompt.
11. Inspect the result at full size and thumbnail size. Iterate once if the photo identity, quiet space, structural color, or torn boundary is weak.
12. Return the final image and one short creative rationale. Include the full prompt only when requested.

## Prompt Construction

Build prompts in this order:

1. Canvas and flat paper surface
2. Attention geometry and photo allocation
3. Scene invariants
4. Retain, merge, omit, transform, and expose decisions
5. Illustration grammar and density
6. Exact accent hue, source-derived shape, and visual function
7. Torn-edge construction
8. Paper, ink, grain, and scan behavior
9. Exact text, if any
10. Hard avoids

For detailed ranges, failure checks, and a reusable prompt template, read [references/design-guide.md](references/design-guide.md).

## Hard Constraints

- Preserve the photograph's identifying subject and key spatial logic.
- Use exactly one added high-chroma hue; natural colors already present in the photo do not count.
- Attach the accent color to source geometry. Do not add arbitrary dots, circles, rectangles, or corner patches.
- Keep the illustration calmer than the photograph, especially for foliage and other dense textures.
- Make the torn photo–paper boundary visibly fibrous, irregular, and narrow.
- Avoid clean digital clipping, sticker borders, uniform deckled frames, curled corners, heavy shadows, layered mockups, dense tracing, generic vintage decoration, and scattered filler marks.
