# Style Recipe

## Visual Language

Make the image feel remembered rather than copied. Use simple composition, tactile paper grain, hand-cut edges, and a restrained travel-zine mood.

## Composition

- Treat the biggest quiet field in the source as the breathing space: sky, sea, road, grass, wall, snow, sand, or water.
- Reduce the scene to a few large forms first.
- Let the main subject stay clear, but simplify secondary details aggressively.
- Keep the main scene visually dominant and the stickers secondary.

## Drawing Style

- Use matte gouache, cut paper, screen print, or risograph-like texture.
- Prefer flat shapes, uneven edges, slight misregistration, and fine paper tooth.
- Use awkward, handmade proportions when they make the image more charming.
- Avoid detailed faces, leaves, wires, fabric folds, tiny machinery, and excessive linework.

## Color

- Derive colors from the source photo.
- Use 5-8 broad color families.
- Keep most colors soft or dusty.
- Preserve one or two memorable accents, such as a red train, yellow dress, green bicycle, blue sea, orange cat, or bright kite.
- Avoid glossy gradients and dramatic lighting.

## Sticker Motifs

Choose six motifs that behave like memory fragments:

- one main subject fragment
- one secondary subject or companion
- one environmental shape
- one structural detail
- one small object
- one atmosphere cue

Examples:

- seaside tram photo: tram corner, pantograph, crossing sign, barrier, cloud, ocean strip
- cat photo: orange cat, black cat, tabby cat, wall edge, cloud, sea horizon
- child kite photo: kite, dog, hat, yellow outfit, spool, meadow flowers

## Keywords

Use three short English phrases. They should sound like labels from a small personal archive, not marketing slogans.

Good examples:

- `SEA TRAM · CLOUD LINE · QUIET CROSSING`
- `KITE RUN · YELLOW DAY · LAKE BREEZE`
- `SIX CATS · BLUE HORIZON · CLOUD BENCH`

Avoid generic words like `TRAVEL`, `BEAUTIFUL`, `MEMORY`, or `PHOTO`.
